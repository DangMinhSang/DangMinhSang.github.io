4 How did the statue get to the United States?
5 Which is TRUE about the statue?

Lời thoại (Transcript):

The Statue of Liberty was a gift of friendship from the people of France to the United States and is recognized as a universal symbol of freedom and democracy. The statue was designed by French sculptor Frédéric-Auguste Bartholdi, while Alexandre-Gustave Eiffel, the designer of the Eiffel Tower, was chosen to engineer the interior metal framework. The statue was constructed in France, finished in 1884, and disassembled into 350 individual pieces packed in 214 crates for transport across the Atlantic Ocean by ship. In the United States, funds were raised to construct a massive granite pedestal base on Bedloe's Island in New York Harbor. The statue rises to 46 meters, and visitors who climb the 354 steps to the crown can enjoy a panoramic view of the harbor.

Lời thoại (Transcript):

Bamboo is one of the fastest growing plants on Earth and an extraordinary natural resource. Although it looks like a tree, bamboo actually belongs to the grass family. Unlike hardwood trees that take decades to mature, bamboo reaches a harvestable size in just 3 to 7 years. Because of its incredible tensile strength—stronger than steel in some tension tests—people in many parts of Asia have traditionally built entire houses, bridges, and scaffolding out of bamboo poles. Modern innovators are now turning bamboo into eco-friendly fabrics, flooring, furniture, and even lightweight bicycle frames. Expanding the commercial cultivation of bamboo could help save millions of trees from deforestation.

Talk / Lecture 3: The Historic BBC Micro Computer

Listen to a museum tour guide talking to visitors about the BBC Micro computer.

File âm thanh: audio/code1_lecture3.mp3
11 Which is TRUE about computers in 1980?
12 After the BBC broadcast the program 'The Mighty Micro'...
13 Why did the BBC decide to commission its own computer?
14 Which company was chosen to build the BBC Micro?
15 How many BBC Micro computers were sold eventually?

Lời thoại (Transcript):

In 1980, computers were mysterious, room-sized machines or expensive scientific devices out of reach for the ordinary household. Seeing that a digital revolution was looming, the British Broadcasting Corporation launched the BBC Computer Literacy Project in 1982. Alongside the popular television series 'The Mighty Micro', the BBC decided they needed an accessible, rugged computer to show people how programming worked. They commissioned Acorn Computers in Cambridge to build the BBC Micro. Although the BBC initially forecasted sales of roughly 12,000 units, the computer became an overwhelming sensation, selling more than 1.5 million units across schools, colleges, and British households, sparking a whole generation of British software engineers.
